# Startup Idea Validation Template

Use this template to structure your validation process before committing significant resources. Fill out each section honestly. The goal is to surface risks early, not to confirm your existing beliefs.

---

## 1. Problem Statement

**Problem statement template**:

> "[Target customer] struggle with [specific problem] when [context/trigger]. This leads to [concrete negative outcome]. Today they solve this by [current workaround], which fails because [root cause of failure]."

**Example**:
> "B2B SaaS founders struggle with understanding their unit economics when they hit their first $100K ARR. This leads to mispriced plans, over-investment in failing channels, and investor conversations they can't support with data. Today they track this in messy spreadsheets, which fails because the formulas don't connect live data and get stale within days."

**Your problem statement**:
> [Write here]

**Validation questions to answer before proceeding**:
- Have at least 10 target customers described this problem in their own words (without prompting)?
- Is there evidence people are actively seeking a solution (Google searches, forum posts, existing products)?
- What is the frequency and severity of the problem? (Daily/weekly vs. quarterly? Business-critical vs. nuisance?)

## 2. Hypothesis Canvas

State your key assumptions explicitly. Rank each by risk (probability of being wrong × severity of impact if wrong).

### Core Hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis | Risk Level | How to Test | Success Threshold |
|---|-----------|------------|-------------|-------------------|
| 1 | Customer hypothesis: "[ICP] has this problem severely enough to act" | High / Med / Low | [Test method] | [Metric] |
| 2 | Solution hypothesis: "Our approach resolves the root cause" | High / Med / Low | [Test method] | [Metric] |
| 3 | Channel hypothesis: "We can reach [ICP] through [channel] at a viable CAC" | High / Med / Low | [Test method] | [Metric] |
| 4 | Revenue hypothesis: "Customers will pay [price] for this" | High / Med / Low | [Test method] | [Metric] |
| 5 | Moat hypothesis: "We have a defensible advantage over copycats" | High / Med / Low | [Test method] | [Metric] |

**Priority**: Test the highest-risk hypotheses first. If hypothesis #1 is wrong, #2-5 don't matter.

### Minimum Experiment Design

For each high-risk hypothesis, define:
- **Experiment**: What exactly are you doing?
- **Duration**: How long will you run it?
- **Cost**: What will it cost (time + money)?
- **Data you'll collect**: Specific, measurable
- **Pass condition**: What result counts as validation?
- **Fail condition**: What result kills this hypothesis?

## 5. Pivot vs. Persevere Decision Criteria

Define your decision points in advance, before emotion is involved.

### Persevere if:
- Core problem confirmed by 20+ interviews
- At least 3 users have paid or made a commitment
- Retention (day-7) is above 30% for early users
- NPS "very disappointed" score is ≥ 40%

### Pivot the solution if:
- Problem is validated but users consistently reject the solution approach
- A different solution keeps coming up unprompted in interviews
- Usage data shows users are hacking around the product to get to value

### Pivot the customer segment if:
- A different customer type is getting much more value than your ICP
- Your ICP doesn't have budget authority or urgency to act
- Referrals consistently come from a different profile than your ICP

### Stop (or significantly restructure) if:
- After 30+ interviews, problem cannot be confirmed
- No one will pay any amount for the solution
- CAC through all tested channels exceeds 5× projected LTV
- Regulatory or technical blocker discovered that changes the fundamental cost structure
